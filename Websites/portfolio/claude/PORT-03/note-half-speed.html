Why I render at half speed

12 September 2026 6 min read Juno Salvi

Every renderer will make you motion blur. Almost all of them make it by guessing: the object was here on frame 12 and there on frame 13, so smear between the two. For a pendulum moving in a straight line that guess is fine. For anything that changes direction inside a frame — which is to say, anything with weight — the guess is visibly wrong, and the wrongness reads as cheapness even to people who cannot name it.

Twice the frames, half the guessing

So I render at 48 frames per second and retime to 24. The renderer now has an actual sample where it used to interpolate, and the blur follows the arc instead of cutting the corner. It costs twice the render time, which on a three-second loop is an evening rather than a week.

  • Render 48fps, retime in the comp, keep the full-rate version for slow-motion crops.
  • Check the fastest two frames at 25% speed; that is where interpolated blur gives itself away.
  • If the budget cannot carry double renders, halve the resolution before you halve the frame rate.
“Render time is the cheapest thing in this studio. My attention is the expensive part.”

When not to bother

Straight-line travel, slow camera moves, anything that will be seen at thumbnail size: the guess is fine, and I take the evening back. The rule is not “always render at half speed”. It is “know which frames are guesses”.
