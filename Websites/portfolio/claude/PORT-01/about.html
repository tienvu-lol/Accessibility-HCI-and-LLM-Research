About

I am Tobias Quill: an interactive developer in Portland, Oregon who keeps a workshop next to a text editor.

I started in 2011 as the junior who was given the footer. Fifteen years later I mostly do two things: design engineering for products that have grown too big to behave consistently, and small built objects that teach something a slide deck cannot.

The through line is stubbornness about the basics. A page should work before the JavaScript arrives, stay usable at 400% zoom, be operable with a keyboard by someone who has never seen a mouse, and open in eleven years without a build step. None of that is interesting on its own. It is interesting when you hold it as a constraint while building something strange.

How I work

I would rather hand you something incomplete on Friday than something perfect in March. Every engagement gets a weekly artefact: a page, a prototype, a plotted sheet.

Keyboard paths, focus order and names go in the first commit. I have never once seen an accessibility pass at the end come in cheaper than doing it first.

HTML, CSS and a single script go a long way. When a project needs more, it gets more, with a note in the README explaining what it buys and what it costs.

Handover includes the decisions, not just the files: what I tried, what I rejected, and the three things I would fix next.

A short history

  1. 2023–now

    Principal interaction engineer, Harborlight

    Design engineering across a scheduling product: component library, keyboard model, and 240 screens taught to behave the same way.

  2. 2019–2023

    Senior front-end engineer, Marrow & Pike

    The public site and the newsroom editor used by 400 staff. Any action reachable in three keystrokes.

  3. 2015–2019

    Interactive developer, Ferrous Studio

    Campaign sites and installations, including one regrettable animated cursor that taught me to budget for crates.

  4. 2011–2015

    Front-end developer, Rook Street Digital

    Melbourne. Where I learned HTML properly, mostly by having it taken apart in code review.

  5. 2007–2011

    BSc, Computer Science

    Plus two years as the student society’s unofficial poster printer, which is where the plotter habit began.

Things people say I am good at

  • Turning an argument about taste into a measurable question.
  • Teaching: workshops, pairing, and writing documentation people actually open.
  • Finishing the unglamorous 20% that makes the glamorous 80% usable.
