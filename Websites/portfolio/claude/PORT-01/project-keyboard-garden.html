Keyboard Garden

A split keyboard whose keys sprout a plant for every thousand words written, with a printed companion card for screen reader users.

  • hardware
  • accessibility

What it is

A birthday present that grew a build log.

The problem

I wanted a writing keyboard that rewarded volume without nagging: no streaks, no notifications, nothing to lose. And I wanted the reward to be legible to someone who cannot see the LEDs, which ruled out the obvious answer.

How I built it

  • Count keystrokes in firmware, in thousands, and never send them anywhere.
  • Each thousand words lights one more leaf on a small side panel, so the state is ambient rather than demanding.
  • Print a companion card with the same state in raised numerals, updated by hand once a week, for a friend who reads with their fingers.
  • Publish the enclosure as a printable file and the firmware patch as a 40-line diff.

Pictures of it working

Three placeholder images. On a real case study these would be photographs and screen recordings.

Where it got to

  • Two units built; one lives on my desk, one with the person it was made for.
  • The build log is the most-read page on this site, which tells you something about what people want from a portfolio.
  • The companion-card idea has since been copied, with permission, into a classroom activity.
“A reward you can ignore for a week is one you keep.”

What I’d do differently

A reward you can ignore for a week is one you keep. The first firmware flashed the whole board at every milestone and I disabled it in two days.

The printed card was meant to be a stopgap and became the best part of the project.

I wrote the log during the build, not after. Every photograph I did not take at the time is a photograph I do not have.
