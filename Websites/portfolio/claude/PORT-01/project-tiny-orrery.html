Tiny Orrery

A hand-cranked SVG solar system for a middle-school science unit. Keyboard operable, printable, and legible at 400% zoom.

  • svg
  • teaching
  • open-source

What it is

Built with a middle-school science teacher over a term.

The problem

The class needed a solar system they could drive, on school laptops from 2016, over a connection that dropped twice an hour. The commercial options were 40 MB of WebGL and needed a login.

How I built it

  • One inline SVG, no images, no fonts to download: the whole page is 34 KB.
  • Crank the model with a slider, the arrow keys, or a page-up jump of one month per press.
  • Every planet is a real element with a real accessible name, so a screen reader can walk the system.
  • A print stylesheet that lays the orbits out as a worksheet, because the laptops were shared and the worksheets were not.

Pictures of it working

Three placeholder images. On a real case study these would be photographs and screen recordings.

Where it got to

  • Three classrooms; the teacher reports it survives a 400% zoom and a flaky projector.
  • Keyboard-only operation was not an accessibility afterthought: it is how most of the class uses it, because the trackpads are worn out.
  • Forked eleven times, mostly by other teachers, which is the only metric I check.
“Every constraint the school gave me made the thing better.”

What I’d do differently

Every constraint the school gave me made the thing better. Nothing about this project needed a framework.

The worksheet got more classroom use than the interactive version in the first month.

Three bugs found in the first lesson, all of them mine, all of them obvious in hindsight.
