# Fidelity notes — Avery North

## Reference evidence

Primary reference: https://www.alexstrohl.com/
Supporting reference: https://www.alexstrohl.com/about
Also verified during the selection stage: /contact, /personal/latest-work, and /links.

The homepage and About page were successfully reopened during implementation. Their readable content confirmed a photographer/filmmaker identity, an extensive image collection, a multicolumn selected-client list, print-shop CTA, newsletter form, biography, distinct contact categories, interview links, and an in-the-field image collection. The homepage is much more image-heavy than a conventional card-based personal site.

## Structural interpretation

The replica prioritizes large imagery, varied gallery proportions, a long image-led homepage, separate story sequences, a latest-work archive, biography, contact categories, print promotion, and newsletter. Galleries use CSS columns on desktop and stack on compact screens. Story pages alternate full-width and paired image studies, with brief/process/credit sections and previous/next navigation.

Warm paper, olive accents, serif editorial headings, and a compact studio wordmark are independently selected visual treatments. The illustrated artwork does not reproduce the original photographer’s photographic texture, composition, subjects, exact crops, or locations. This is a structural study, not a pixel-matched visual clone.

## Original substitutions

Avery North is fictional. All biography, employer names, project stories, client-like names, testimonials, recognition, and contact addresses are invented for this package and labeled as illustrative. Twelve original SVG landscape illustrations replace all photography and film stills. They are deliberately identified as placeholders, not real photographs or proof of a real shoot. The SVGs are local and include small placeholder labels.

## Research extensions

Sticky utility/header navigation, searchable/filterable work and journal listings, four local project essays, three local journal articles, Services, CV, Toolkit, FAQ, Press, a 404 document, manual testimonial carousel, demo booking/inquiry/licensing workflows, theme switching, clipboard actions, a static availability badge and real studio-local clock, and a demo NDA gate are research additions. Do not treat them as observations of the original.

The original’s newsletter and print purchase destinations are represented locally with non-transmitting success states. No commerce platform, mailing list, image licensing contract, calendar, payment, account, or backend is recreated. The print modal supplies selectable formats and illustrative prices only. Moving-image services are described, but no borrowed film/video is embedded; the portfolio uses still placeholders rather than pretending to reproduce a film player or 3D experience.

## Scope and code

Twenty-one full HTML pages use CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript. No source code from Alex Strohl’s site is copied, and its internal architecture is not inferred from rendered text. No React, build tooling, database, or backend logic is present. Layout code is new; the reusable behavior foundation is also used by the companion Jamie Vale site, while the design and information hierarchy are separate.

## Verification limit

Browser host access was unavailable; attempting native Safari returned that computer-use permissions were not granted. No rendered comparison or live interaction test was possible. Typography, spacing, sticky behavior, breakpoints, cropping, focus/zoom behavior, and exact fidelity remain unverified in a real browser. The included source checks should not be interpreted as rendered QA or accessibility certification.
