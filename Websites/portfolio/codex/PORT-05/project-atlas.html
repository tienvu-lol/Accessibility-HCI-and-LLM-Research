Atlas travel planner.

A calmer way to plan a few days away.

Role
Design & frontend concept
Year
2026
Status
Fictional study
brief

Planning without the spreadsheet

People need to compare possibilities without losing the plan they already made. The concept brings a day-by-day itinerary and a short list of saved places into one readable view.

A travel-planning concept that replaces scattered lists with a clear, flexible itinerary. This is a fictional design exploration, not a commissioned product or a claim of measured business results.

process

From a question to a working pattern.

01

Understand the task

Write a clear primary journey. Identify where a person must choose, wait, or recover from a mistake before drawing the polished screen.

02

Explore the structure

Compare a few low-fidelity arrangements. Keep related information together and separate secondary actions from the next useful step.

03

Build the details

Prototype keyboard paths, narrow screens, error messages, and empty states. Document the decisions that another person would otherwise have to guess.

studies

A closer look.

reflection

What I’d take forward.

The useful part of this exploration is the hierarchy: one clear primary task, a visible route back, and consistent feedback when something changes.

Next, I would test the concept with representative users, review the vocabulary, and compare the keyboard journey against the visual one. No user testing has been conducted for this fictional project.
