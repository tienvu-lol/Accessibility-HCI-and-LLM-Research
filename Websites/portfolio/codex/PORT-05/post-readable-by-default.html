Start with a smaller question

A few practical CSS decisions that make long-form content easier to live with. Before changing the surface of a screen, write down the task it needs to support. A clear task gives the visual work something concrete to respond to.

part 1

Let structure carry the meaning

A good hierarchy explains what belongs together. Use spacing, readable labels, and a predictable order before reaching for decoration. Make the most useful information the easiest thing to find.

part 2

Design the awkward states

The empty list, the long title, the invalid field, and the narrow viewport are part of the product. They reveal where a pattern is flexible and where it only works for the ideal example.

part 3

Leave a useful trail

Write down the decisions a future collaborator would otherwise have to rediscover. A short note attached to a concrete example is often more helpful than a long abstract rule.
