A coat built from six undyed wools

At Hollowbrook Mills, a winter coat is assembled from the natural shades of six fleeces, and nothing is dyed.

By Ilsabet Corrow / Feb 14, 2031 / 6 min read

Gallery

The coat begins in a sorting shed, where six wools are laid out side by side: a cream, a fawn, a pale grey, a brown, a charcoal and a near-black. At Hollowbrook Mills, a fictional mill in this sample, each fleece is spun and woven into its own cloth before anything is cut.

Six cloths, one weight

Because the colours are the fibre itself, the finished coat reads as a quiet patchwork of panels. The makers say the hardest part is matching the weight of six different cloths so that the coat hangs as one. Natural wools differ in crimp and springiness, and a charcoal fleece may take up more space in the loom than a cream one.

Nothing is dyed, so every seam is a decision about where one wool ends and the next begins.

Cutting and joining

Panels are cut from paper patterns, then tested in a plain calico before the good cloth is touched. Joins fall along the lines where the eye already expects a break: the yoke, the side seam and the sleeve head. Each seam is pressed open and finished by hand.

Care and repair

The mill sends a small card with each coat explaining how to air it, brush it and store it with cedar. A repair service is offered at a sample counter in the mill yard, and patches are cut from the offcuts of the original cloths.
