Program description

Investigate life at every scale, from the systems within a cell to the relationships within an ecosystem. This fictional degree blends foundational science, field observation, and guided inquiry.

You might like this program if…

  • You enjoy asking how living systems work.
  • You want to connect laboratory methods and field observations.
  • You value careful evidence and collaborative learning.

Entrance to major

Sample progression: complete introductory biology, chemistry, and a quantitative reasoning course, then meet with an academic adviser. These are illustrative requirements, not actual admission guidance.

Requirements for the major

Study cell biology, ecology, genetics, chemistry, and quantitative methods. Choose a general biology or ecology pathway for your advanced coursework.

General education and university requirements

Build communication, ethical reasoning, and an understanding of human communities alongside your scientific studies.

Program learning objectives

  • Frame a biological question and evaluate evidence.
  • Explain connections across levels of biological organization.
  • Communicate scientific findings clearly and responsibly.

Suggested academic plan

General biology option

Year 1: introductory sciences and writing. Year 2: genetics and quantitative methods. Year 3: advanced biology and electives. Year 4: capstone inquiry and remaining requirements.

Ecology option

Year 1: foundations in biology and chemistry. Year 2: ecology and statistics. Year 3: field methods and environmental systems. Year 4: an integrative field project.

Career paths

Explore interests in environmental work, laboratory science, science communication, or further study. No employment or admission outcomes are promised.
