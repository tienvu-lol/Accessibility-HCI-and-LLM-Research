Why the harbor’s oldest ferry route may go quiet by spring

Rising fuel costs and a retiring fleet leave a commuter lifeline with two boats and no replacement budget.

By Dev Anand Okoro · Larkspur Public Media

November 9, 2026 · 5 min listen

Rising fuel costs and a retiring fleet have put the county’s oldest commuter ferry in doubt. Two boats remain in service, a third is in dry dock, and the county’s capital budget holds nothing for a replacement.

Riders interviewed on the early crossing described a route that is slower than a bridge but quieter and more predictable. Local employers say they would lose workers without it.

Host: On the harbor’s north side, the oldest ferry route in the county has two boats left and a budget meeting on Thursday.

Reporter: I rode the 6:40 with about forty commuters. Most said the same thing: if the boat goes, the drive adds an hour each way.

Host: The county says it will decide by spring.
