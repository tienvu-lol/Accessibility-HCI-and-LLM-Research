A seed library in Ostermoor lends out tomatoes and asks for stories back

Borrowers return seeds each autumn, along with a note about how the plants fared.

By Ketevi Marlowe-Sunde (invented reporter)

OSTERMOOR, Ardenmere (fictional) - Behind the reading room of the Ostermoor branch library, a wooden card catalogue holds no cards. Its drawers hold paper envelopes of seeds, each labelled in pencil: dwarf beans, lemon cucumbers, a tomato known only as "Grandmother's Stripe."

A loan with a story attached

Librarian Dessa Whitlock-Ferne explains that borrowers promise to return a few seeds at season's end. "We also ask for a paragraph," she said. "Did it sulk? Did it sprawl? That paragraph is the real catalogue." The archive of paragraphs now fills three binders.

The library says the project began with a single shoebox and has grown to four hundred invented varieties, all of them fictional for the purposes of this sample.
