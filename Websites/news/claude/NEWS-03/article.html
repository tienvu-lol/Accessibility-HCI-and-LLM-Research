National

Flood-control vote splits river counties as autumn storms return

A $1.4 billion levee plan passed the state senate by one vote, and the counties that would pay for it are not done arguing.

By Marisol Quint · Larkspur Public Media

November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Demo: every sample story shares the same placeholder article body below. Only the headline, summary, section and date change.

Senators approved the plan on a 21-20 vote late Monday, sending a $1.4 billion package of levees, culverts and buyouts to the governor. Supporters call it overdue. Opponents in the river counties say they are being asked to carry a bill for protection they may never see.

The county that would receive the largest new levee also has the smallest tax base. “We are the ones who get wet,” said a commissioner there, “and we are the ones who cannot afford to stay dry.” A state analysis says local shares would phase in over fifteen years.

Engineers who reviewed the plan for the legislature praised its wetland buffers but questioned the cost of two downstream gates. They said the gates could be delayed without raising risk to nearby neighborhoods in most scenarios.

Forecasters expect a wetter than average winter. If a storm of the size seen three years ago returns before construction begins, emergency managers say the region would rely on the same temporary barriers it used then.

The governor has until the end of the month to sign the measure, veto it, or let it become law without a signature. A spokesperson said only that the office is “reading every page.”
