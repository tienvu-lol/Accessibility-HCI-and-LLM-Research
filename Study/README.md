# LLM UI Repair: Accessibility vs. Usability Trade-offs

Experiment protocol. This document is the single source of truth for the repair
study. It is written to be executable by someone (or some agent) who has not read
the conversation that produced it.

**Research question.** Does a WCAG-focused LLM repair loop improve accessibility at
the cost of Nielsen usability, and where do the two conflict?

**Revision.** v3 -- 6 repair arms, 60 sites (the full post-reduction dataset).

---

## 1. Terminology (read this first)

Two different sets of models are involved. Confusing them invalidates the study.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Generator group** | The model group that *built* a replica website: `claude`, `codex`, or `open-source`. Historical. Fixed. Governed by `Websites/MODEL_ALLOCATION.md`. **Not a variable in this experiment.** |
| **Repair arm** | One of the 6 models that *repairs* websites in this study. The secondary independent variable. **Unrelated to generator group.** |
| **Condition** | One of 4 repair prompts: `naive`, `wcag`, `nielsen`, `both`. The primary independent variable. |
| **Variant** | One repaired output = (site x arm x condition). |
| **Item** | One (site, arm) pair = one judge ranking call covering that arm's 4 variants. |

A site generated by Claude may be repaired by all six arms. Generator group is
balanced across the dataset by construction and is not analyzed.

## 2. Dataset

### The replica dataset (as of the v2.0 slot reduction, 2026-11-19)

60 static replica websites built under `Websites/Rules.md`. **All 60 are in the
study** -- there is no site subsample.

- **10 categories x 6 replicas**: university, healthcare, company, e-commerce,
  news, government, fashion, weather, portfolio, restaurant
- Each category splits **2 claude / 2 codex / 2 open-weight** by generator group,
  so generator group is perfectly balanced and cannot confound anything
- Stable IDs `<PREFIX>-01` .. `<PREFIX>-06`; slot range implies group
  (01-02 claude, 03-04 codex, 05-06 open-weight)
- Path: `Websites/<category>/<group>/<ID>/`
- Pages per replica: median 11, mean 11.5, range 5-23
- `nonprofit` and `church` are registered in `Websites/metadata/assignments.csv`
  but **not built** (20 rows, `approved`/`planned`). Out of scope.

40 replicas (former slots 03/06/08/09) were retired to `archive/` on
2026-11-19 and are **not** part of this study. See
`Websites/MODEL_ALLOCATION.md` §2.0 and `archive/README.md`.

### Low-form-density subgroup -- pre-register this

Applying a measurement-validity bar of `index.html` >= 2 KB **and** some page with
>= 3 form controls, **44 of 60 pass**. The 16 that fail do so almost entirely for
lack of a form-dense page:

```
codex:        CORP-03 FASH-03 REST-03 CORP-04 REST-04 WX-04
open-weight:  ECOM-05 FASH-05 NEWS-05 PORT-05 CORP-06 ECOM-06
              HOSP-06 NEWS-06 REST-06 UNI-06
```

**Keep all 60.** Dropping the 16 would re-break the 2/2/2 generator balance (it
would leave 20 claude / 14 codex / 10 open-weight). The 2-page slice rule still
works on them -- it just selects a page with few or no form controls.

Pre-register these 16 as a **low-form-density subgroup**: report form-related
criteria (1.3.1, 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 4.1.2 and heuristics 5 and 9) for the 44 separately,
and all other criteria across the full 60. Record the flag as a column in
`sample/sites.csv`.

### Page slice

Repairing whole sites would be ~40,000 model calls. Not feasible.

**Each variant is a fixed 2-page slice**, enumerated per site in the `slice_files`
column of `sample/sites.csv`:
1. `index.html`
2. the page in that replica with the most form controls (`form_page`)
3. its stylesheet(s) (`stylesheet`) -- contrast and focus fixes live there

**Do not hardcode `css/styles.css`.** 58 of 60 sites use it; `PORT-03` and
`PORT-04` use `assets/styles.css`. Read `slice_files` instead. (Those two also
violate the required-file list in `Websites/Rules.md` that `tools/qc/qc.js`
enforces, which is a pre-existing dataset inconsistency, not a study decision.)

`baseline/<site_id>/` holds a **full copy** of the replica, not an extract --
pages reference `assets/`, `js/` and `css/`, and broken images would corrupt judge
screenshots and human rating views. The slice says what gets *repaired and
scored*, not what exists on disk. 60 copies is ~11 MB.

**Why 2 pages and not just `index.html`:** across the 60 replicas there are 79
form controls on home pages and 1,326 on other pages -- 94% are off the home page. A home-page-only slice would miss form labelling,
error identification and help text (1.3.1, 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 4.1.2) -- which is both a
large share of WCAG and the exact surface where WCAG collides with Nielsen
heuristics 5 and 9. Record the chosen second page per site in `sample/sites.csv`.

## 3. Design

```
60 sites
  x 6 repair arms
  x 4 conditions (naive, wcag, nielsen, both)
  = 1,440 repaired variants
  + 60 untouched baselines
  = 1,500 variants to evaluate
```

| Quantity | Count |
|---|---|
| Sites | 60 (all of them) |
| Repair arms | 6 |
| Conditions | 4 |
| Repaired variants | 1,440 |
| Variants evaluated (incl. baselines) | 1,500 |
| Repair calls (3 iterations, upper bound) | 4,320 |
| Judge calls | 360 |
| Human validation items | 30 |

### Repair arms

| Arm | Model | Family | Serving |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) | Claude | API |
| A2 | Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) | Claude | API |
| A3 | Astra | record before S0 | API |
| A4 | Sol 6.1 | record before S0 | API |
| A5 | DeepSeek | DeepSeek | hosted API |
| A6 | Qwen 3.6 | Qwen | hosted API |

Settled 2026-11-19. Exact model ids, version strings and base URLs live in

**A1 and A2 share a family.** This is deliberate and useful -- a capability-tier
contrast with family held constant. Report it as such; do not describe the six as
six independent families. They span five.

**Verify before committing A3 and A4:** context window sufficient for 2 HTML pages
+ CSS + criteria JSON (~15-20K tokens in), and output capacity for ~10-15K tokens.
Record exact model id and version in `config/repair-allocation.json`.

### Judge

**Gemini.** Not a repair arm, and not one of the generator groups
(claude / codex / open-source), so it has no self-preference on any arm. It has
vision for the screenshots the ranking task requires.

### Serving: the binding constraint

The four API arms are cheap. The two open-weight arms decide the schedule.

| Serving | Open-weight cost per call | Feasible sites |
|---|---|---|
| All 6 via hosted APIs | ~70-90s | **60 comfortably** |
| DeepSeek + Qwen self-hosted on VT ARC Falcon | ~5min, 4 concurrent | ~30 |

Self-hosted at 60 sites: 2 arms x 60 x 4 x 3 = 1,440 calls x 5min / 4 concurrent
= **30 hours**. Infeasible.

**Run A5 and A6 through hosted providers.** Identical weights; `eligibility_note`
in `Websites/pipelines/open-source/models.json` already treats serving environment
as recorded metadata, so this is a logged provenance change in
`Websites/metadata/change-log.md`, not a design change. Evidence that self-hosting
breaks on repair workloads is already in `pipelines/open-source/runs/`:
`SUPERSEDED-HOSP-09-repair-stage-timeout`, `UNI-09-repair-stage-timeout`,
`NEWS-08-repair-cancelled`, `PORT-08-repair-cancelled`.

If self-hosting is mandatory, cut to **30 sites (3 per category)** and depth-nest
the two open-weight arms: depth 1 on all 30, depth 3 on a stratified 10.

### Repair loop

Per (site, arm, condition): the model is given the 2-page slice plus its
condition's criteria, and in **one call** returns (a) its evaluation as JSON and
(b) the rewritten files. Evaluate-and-repair stay in the same model and the same
call -- a model's self-evaluate-then-repair loop is what is being studied.

Loop until the normalized output hash equals the previous iteration's
(convergence) or **3 iterations**, whichever first. Expect 25-30% of runs to
converge early.

### Conditions

| Condition | Context supplied |
|---|---|
| `naive` | none -- "improve this page" |
| `wcag` | WCAG 2.2 criteria as structured JSON |
| `nielsen` | Nielsen's 10 heuristics as structured JSON |
| `both` | both; WCAG takes precedence on conflict |

`criteria/wcag-2.2.json` holds **38 success criteria** (19 Level A, 19 AA),
derived from the w3c/wcag normative source bundled in `files.zip`. Each entry
carries the W3C `text` plus a study-authored `static_html_test` line, clearly
separated. Criteria excluded by scope -- media, timing, seizures, pointer
gestures, authentication, redundant entry, and all AAA -- are enumerated in the
file's `excluded` block with reasons, which is what the poster's coverage
statement cites.

### Held constant across all arms

Any of these differing between arms is a confound.

| Hold constant | May differ (record it) |
|---|---|
| Prompt text, byte-for-byte | model id + version string |
| Criteria JSON | serving environment / provider |
| **Temperature 0** (see below) | quantization, if any |
| `max_tokens` (32000 everywhere) | concurrency / throughput |
| Reasoning mode (**disabled everywhere**) | wall-clock timing |
| Iteration cap + convergence rule | |
| Page slice | |
| Retry policy + truncation guard | |
| Output format spec | |

Two specific traps:

- `Websites/pipelines/open-source/models.json` sets `max_tokens` 16000 for
  Llama-3.3-70B and 32000 for others. **Uniform 32000 across all six arms.** A
  lower cap truncates more, and truncation reads as accessibility improvement.
- That same file notes reasoning was left enabled for *generation* by project
  decision. **Disable it for all six repair arms.** Enabled in some and not others
  confounds model with reasoning mode. Scope statement: "we evaluate non-reasoning
  repair." This matters more at 6 arms, where the arms differ in whether they ship
  reasoning on by default.

**Temperature is 0, not a default sampling temperature.** The study runs **one
call per (site, arm, condition)** -- there are no replicates. At any temperature
above 0, the difference between two conditions on the same site is the prompt
effect *plus* sampling noise, and with n=1 per cell those cannot be separated.
Removing that noise would require k replicates per cell, i.e. 4,320 x k calls.

Temperature 0 also keeps the convergence check working: the loop halts when
iteration N's normalized hash equals N-1's, and at a sampling temperature the
model emits slightly different text every pass even with nothing left to change,
so convergence would never fire and every run would burn all 3 iterations.

Cost: temperature 0 is less representative of how people actually prompt. State it
as scope -- "we evaluate deterministic-decoding repair" -- not as a confound.
Caveat: temperature 0 is not bit-identical across API calls (batching, hardware
nondeterminism), so normalize aggressively before hashing. If convergence almost
never fires, that is the cause, not a bug.

**Orchestration is deterministic code, not a model.** The generation pipeline used
model-driven orchestration (`"mode": "orchestrated-api-execution"`, 28 turns). Do
not carry that in. A model orchestrator makes unlogged judgment calls that vary by
arm, injecting uncontrolled variance into the independent variable.

Pin everything in `config/repair-allocation.json` and have the runner read
parameters from that file, so a confound is impossible by construction.

## 4. Phase 1 -- Repair generation

### Runner requirements

- **Resumable.** Skip any output whose `status.json` says `ok`. A crash must cost
  minutes, not hours.
- **Truncation guard.** Reject any response missing `</html>` or whose text-node
  character count is below 50% of input. Retry up to 2x, then mark `failed`.
  **Never keep a truncated output** -- a page that lost half its content shows a
  dramatic false accessibility win.
- **Convergence.** Whitespace-normalized hash of the output vs. previous iteration.
- **Logging.** Raw response, token usage, latency, model version per call.
- **Per-arm concurrency**, read from config. Arms have different rate limits;
  one slow arm must not throttle the others.
- **Measure 5 real repair calls per arm first** and set concurrency from observed
  throughput, not from estimates.

### Expected cost (all six API-served)

| | Calls | Wall clock |
|---|---|---|
| 6 arms x 60 sites x 4 conditions x 3 iters | 4,320 | |
| after convergence stops (~75%) | ~3,240 | |
| at ~80s/call, 16 concurrent, arms in parallel | | **~5-7h** |

Arms run concurrently on separate endpoints, so wall clock is set by the slowest
arm, not the sum.

## 5. Phase 2 -- Accessibility evaluation

**Applies to all 1,500 variants, every iteration. No LLM. No humans.**
~6,600 page-versions x 2 breakpoints, ~2.5h at 8 workers.

Fork `Websites/tools/qc/qc.js`. It already runs Playwright + axe-core at 1280 and
375 with the right WCAG tag set. Four changes:

1. **Gate -> meter.** `qc.js` calls `fail()` on every violation and `exit(1)`. A
   scorer records; it does not judge. Strip the fail/exit logic.
2. **Keep `passes` and `incomplete`**, not just `violations`.
3. **Single 2-page slice**, not `walk(dir)` over the whole site.
4. **Drop `best-practice`** from the primary metric -- it is not WCAG. Keep as a
   secondary column.

### Normalization -- mandatory

**Never report raw violation counts.** A model that deletes content shows a huge
false improvement. Report `violations / (violations + passes)` and violations per
100 elements, and carry text-node character count as a covariate. Any variant
losing >20% of its text is a truncation artifact, excluded before analysis and
reported as an exclusion count per arm per condition.

### Behavioral probes (~2.6h to build)

Keyboard navigation and screen-reader structure are **executed and observed**, not
judged. These run on all 1,500.

**Tab traversal** -- press Tab repeatedly, record `document.activeElement` each step
(tag, id, text, DOM index, bounding rect). One trace yields four criteria:

| Signal | Criterion |
|---|---|
| focus stops advancing | 2.1.2 keyboard trap |
| DOM index not monotonic | 2.4.3 focus order |
| clickable elements never focused | 2.1.1 keyboard |
| first stop is skip link and it lands on `#main-content` | 2.4.1 bypass blocks |
| screenshot-diff of the element rect, unfocused vs focused | 2.4.7 focus visible |

Use screenshot-diff for focus visibility, not computed `outline` -- CSS fakes a
focus ring many ways. Diff in memory; never persist per-stop screenshots.

**Accessibility tree** via CDP (`page.context().newCDPSession(page)` ->
`Accessibility.getFullAXTree`), not `page.accessibility.snapshot()` which is
thinner and semi-legacy in Playwright ^1.65. The AX tree is what a screen reader
consumes. Compute:

- accessible name present / empty / overlong, per interactive node
- **total accessible-name chars / interactive node count** -- the screen-reader
  verbosity metric, and the best single number for the conflict story
- heading outline from `role=heading` + level -> skipped levels (1.3.1)
- landmark structure, role mismatches

**Heading outline sequence** (0.5h), **reflow at 375** (already in `qc.js`).

If time-constrained, cut in this order: target size (2.5.8), text spacing (1.4.12),
focus visibility. Keep tab traversal and heading outline.

### Probe verification (~45 min, not a rating study)

The probes are deterministic observations, so they need **verification against
ground truth**, not kappa. A buggy probe is consistently buggy; agreement
statistics would not catch it.

Pick 10 pages, tab through each manually in a real browser, write down the stops,
and diff against the probe's output. Methods sentence:

> Keyboard and focus probes were verified against manual traversal on 10 randomly
> selected pages, agreeing on all tab stops and flagging the same focus-visibility
> failures.

axe itself needs no validation -- it is an established tool. Cite it.

### Capture axe `incomplete`

`qc.js` currently discards it. `incomplete` is axe saying "a human must decide
this." Capture it, and use it to **route** the manual review sample rather than
picking pages arbitrarily. If WCAG-only repair increases `incomplete`, that is
itself a finding: markup automated tools cannot adjudicate.

### Coverage statement for the poster

| Method | Criteria |
|---|---|
| axe | 1.1.1 presence, 1.3.1 partial, 1.4.3, 2.4.4 partial, 3.1.1, 4.1.2 partial, 3.3.2 partial |
| Probes | 2.1.1, 2.1.2, 2.4.3, 2.4.7, 2.4.1, 1.4.10, 1.3.1 outline |
| LLM judge (Phase 3) | alt-text form, heading/label descriptiveness, error message quality, content order |
| Real screen reader | ~6 pages, qualitative illustration only |
| Out of scope | 1.2.x media, 2.2.x timing, 3.3.8 auth -- sites are static by protocol |

Do not claim full WCAG coverage. Claim an enumerated subset with a documented
detection method per criterion. If a "% of WCAG detectable by automation" figure
goes on the poster, cite a source -- published figures vary widely.

## 6. Phase 3 -- Usability evaluation (LLM judge)

**360 calls.** One per (site, arm). Arms are never mixed within a call.

### Input

The 5 variants for that (site, arm) (4 repaired conditions + the unrepaired baseline), **shuffled and relabelled V1-V5**. Record the
mapping in `blinding_key.csv` and never expose it to the judge. Each variant
supplies its 2 HTML pages + screenshots. Strip any condition markers from HTML and
filenames.

### Task

For **each of 14 questions separately**, rank V1-V5 best to worst. **Ties are
explicitly permitted** -- a judge forced to totally order 5 items never ties while
humans tie 30-50% of the time, and that marginal mismatch caps achievable kappa
even when the judge is tracking humans perfectly.

- Q1-Q10: Nielsen's 10 heuristics
- Q11: image alt text -- which better describes each image's purpose? (fails:
  filenames, "image of...", >15 words)
- Q12: headings and labels -- which better describe what follows?
- Q13: error messages -- which tells you what to do, not just that something is wrong?
- Q14: content order -- reading top to bottom, which makes more sense?

Q11-Q14 cover judgment-requiring accessibility criteria at full scale, validated by
the same human subset as the heuristics. Judge at temperature 0.

### Output

One call returns 14 rankings. 360 calls x 14 = 5,040 rankings.

Un-blind with the key, then derive any pairwise contrast. The primary contrast is
**`wcag` vs `both`**: whichever was ranked higher wins that question for that item.

### Scope note

The judge sees HTML plus screenshots. It can detect that alt text is a filename,
empty, or 40 words long; it **cannot** verify alt text accurately describes a
specific image. Phrase the criterion as *alt-text form and contextual
appropriateness*, not accuracy.

## 7. Phase 4 -- Human validation

**30 items.** Run after all 360 judge calls complete.

### The draw -- rotated, not every arm per site

**An item is a `(site, arm)` pair, not a site.** The judge produced 360 items
(60 sites x 6 arms); human validation draws a subset of those 360. Drawing a site
does **not** oblige you to take all 6 of its arms. The unrated arms were still
ranked by the judge and still enter the analysis -- they simply are not part of
the validity check. The validation subset does not have to mirror the structure of
the main study; it only needs enough independent observations to estimate
agreement.

Taking all 6 arms of 6 sites (36 items) looks like more data but is less: kappa's
interval is driven by independent **sites**, not items. At an intraclass
correlation of 0.2, 36 items from 6 sites give n_eff = 18, while 30 items from 10
sites give n_eff = 21.4 -- more information for 20% less rater work.

**The draw: 1 site per category x 3 of the 6 arms, rotated so each arm appears 5
times.** The rotation is fixed, not random, so there is nothing to justify:

| Category | Site (seed 20261119) | Arms |
|---|---|---|
| university | UNI-04 | A1 A2 A3 |
| healthcare | HOSP-05 | A4 A5 A6 |
| company | CORP-05 | A1 A2 A4 |
| e-commerce | ECOM-02 | A3 A5 A6 |
| news | NEWS-01 | A1 A3 A4 |
| government | GOV-03 | A2 A5 A6 |
| fashion | FASH-06 | A1 A4 A5 |
| weather | WX-03 | A2 A3 A6 |
| portfolio | PORT-01 | A1 A5 A6 |
| restaurant | REST-02 | A2 A3 A4 |

10 categories x 3 arms = **30 items**, 420 judgments per rater, ~2.75h. All 10
categories covered, every arm 5 times, 3 items per site. Generator mix of the 10
drawn sites: 4 claude / 3 codex / 3 open-weight.

Recorded in `sample/human_items.csv`. The 30 are a **strict subset of the 360** --
different items would mean no shared units and no kappa at all.

**Site draw rule:** within each category the site is drawn (seeded) from that
category's **form-capable** sites only -- those outside the low-form-density
subgroup in section 2. Otherwise Q13 (error messages) is unanswerable on some
items and raters default to "tie", artificially deflating kappa for that question.
Limitation to state: the validation sample over-represents form-rich sites
relative to the full 60, so kappa may generalize imperfectly to the
low-form-density subgroup.

Per-arm kappa at 5 appearances is meaningless, but it was equally meaningless at
10. Kappa is pooled across arms.

### What a rater does

Not ranking. For each item they see **2 variants only** -- `wcag` and `both` --
rendered side by side, labelled A and B, left/right randomized **per rater** and
recorded. No condition names anywhere. The other 2 variants are never shown.

Per item, 14 rows: A / B / Tie, plus an optional note filled only when the
difference is sharp. Those notes become poster examples.

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Raters | 3 (the research team) |
| Judgments per rater | 30 x 14 = 420 (~2.75h) |
| Total human judgments | 1,260 |
| Variant renders needed | 60 (120 pages) |

Rules: per-rater item shuffle; nobody sees judge output until all three have
submitted -- assign one person to hold it; rubric anchors written **before** any
scoring and never revised mid-way.

### Where rating happens

A local rating harness, one self-contained HTML file per rater, opened from disk:

```
Study/human/harness/rater_R1.html   pre-seeded with R1's randomization
                    rater_R2.html
                    rater_R3.html
```

Two `file://` iframes side by side, a page toggle (index / form page), the 14
questions below, and a Next button. `file://` renders faithfully because the
generation protocol forbids network requests and root-absolute paths -- `qc.js`
fails a replica for either.

The harness enforces blinding (iframe paths resolve through a lookup table;
nothing visible says `wcag` or `both`), per-rater left/right randomization,
per-rater item shuffle, **localStorage autosave after every item** (420 judgments
is 2.75h of work; a browser crash without it is unrecoverable), and CSV export in
the `human_ratings.csv` schema.

`tools/previewer/` is not reusable -- it is a C# WinForms app producing a Windows
`.exe`.

Google Sheets is for the combined result, **not for collection**. Reading from a
browser and typing into a Sheet costs transcription errors across 420 rows and
leaks blinding the moment a condition column is visible. Harness -> three CSVs ->
concatenate -> `Study/human/human_ratings.csv` (committed).

### Validity math

Computed **per question** (14 times), over 30 items.

**1. Collapse 3 raters to a majority.** A 1-1-1 three-way split has no majority:
exclude from Cohen's kappa, report the count. Pre-register this rule.

**2. Fleiss' kappa** (do the 3 raters agree with each other?) -- validates the
*rubric*, not the judge.

```
P_i = (sum_j n_ij^2 - 3) / 6          per item, 3 raters
P_bar = mean(P_i)
P_e = sum_j p_j^2                      p_j = overall share of category j
kappa = (P_bar - P_e) / (1 - P_e)
```

**3. Cohen's kappa** (does the judge track the humans?) -- judge's derived pairwise
verdict vs. human majority.

```
kappa = (p_o - p_e) / (1 - p_e)
p_o = fraction of items where they agree
p_e = sum over categories of (judge rate x human rate)
```

Worked example, one question, 28 usable items: judge says `both`/`wcag`/tie on
20/6/2, humans on 19/7/2, agreeing on 22. p_o = 0.786, p_e = 0.543,
**kappa = 0.53**. Note 79% raw agreement maps to kappa 0.53 -- most of that 79% was
chance, which is why percent agreement is never reported alone.

**4. Confidence interval -- bootstrap clustered by site.** 30 items come from only
10 sites; items from the same site are correlated. Resample the 10 **sites** with
replacement, taking each site's items as a block, recompute kappa, 2,000 times,
take the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles. The closed-form SE assumes independence you do
not have. Expect roughly +/-0.30 per question; pooled across questions, ~+/-0.10.

**5. Gate.**

| Fleiss | Cohen | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| >= 0.40 | >= 0.40 | **use** -- analyze all 360 items' rankings for this question |
| >= 0.40 | < 0.40 | rubric fine, judge unreliable -> drop from claims |
| < 0.40 | any | rubric broken -> drop; the judge was not the problem |

Expect 8-11 of 14 to pass. Report the full table including failures: "our judge did
not track human raters on aesthetic and minimalist design (kappa = 0.18), so we make
no claim there" is stronger than burying it in an average.

**Optional, cheap:** run the judge in *pairwise* mode on just these 30 items (30
extra calls). The judge ranks 4 while humans compare 2, so some disagreement is
instrument artifact. Pairwise-vs-pairwise is the cleaner validity number, and
pairwise-vs-ranking quantifies the context effect.

## 8. Phase 5 -- Analysis

Run by a separate agent against the CSVs in section 9.

**Gate first.** Apply the kappa table. Only surviving questions proceed.

**Usability.** Per (arm x surviving question): Friedman test across the 4
conditions, n=60 blocks. 6 arms x ~11 surviving questions ~= **66 omnibus tests**
-> Benjamini-Hochberg. Post-hoc pairwise Wilcoxon with Holm **only where Friedman
is significant**. Report mean rank per condition and Kendall's W, not just p-values.

> Note: at 6 arms the multiple-comparison burden roughly doubles versus the 3-arm
> design. Marginal effects that would have survived at 3 arms may not. Expect this
> before seeing the output; do not relax the correction afterward.

**Accessibility.** Paired deltas per (site, arm) between conditions. Wilcoxon
signed-rank -- violation counts are not normal, do not use a t-test. All 6 pairwise
condition contrasts are free here since every condition is measured at full census.

**Family contrast.** A1 vs A2 (Opus 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5) is a capability-tier
comparison with family held constant. Report it separately from the across-family
comparisons.

**The headline figure.** 360 points (60 sites x 6 arms), colored by arm:
- x = delta normalized violation rate (`both` - `wcag`)
- y = net usability rank advantage (`both` - `wcag`) across surviving questions

Four quadrants; the lower-left is the trade-off. Count it, report the share.

**Conflict cases -> qualitative.** Filter for delta violations improved AND usability
lost on >= 2 questions. Rank by magnitude, read the top 15 diffs by hand. 3-4
concrete examples beat any aggregate chart on a poster.

**Screen reader sample.** ~6 pages from the sharpest conflict cases, one person,
VoiceOver, ~45 min. Illustration only -- do not call it a sample. If the time is not
there, drop it and state in Limitations that no assistive-technology testing was
performed.

### Results template

> Across 60 replica websites and 6 repair models spanning 5 families, WCAG-only
> repair reduced detected violations by X% but ranked lower than WCAG+Nielsen on n

> under WCAG-only repair, indicating increased screen-reader verbosity. k of 360
> site-arm pairs showed the trade-off directly.

## 12. Critical gates and failure modes

**Gate: baseline ceiling check (S3).** `Websites/Rules.md:125` warns that replicas
were built to a WCAG-oriented target, so baselines may start near-clean and leave
no room to show repair. Score the 60 baselines **before** generating 1,440
variants. If median violations are near zero, respond then -- lean on normalized
rates, behavioral probes and verbosity, which have more headroom.

**Degenerate baselines.** The worst offenders (all former slot 09, median 875
bytes) were retired to `archive/` in the v2.0 reduction. The smallest
surviving index is `HOSP-06` at 1,553 bytes. Do not drop it -- it is covered by the
pre-registered low-form-density subgroup in section 2.

**Truncation.** The most common and most dangerous failure. Guard it in the runner,
exclude at analysis, report counts per arm.

**Self-judging.** The judge must sit outside all six arms' families. Otherwise it
inflates one arm; measure the bias if unavoidable.

**Blinding.** Judge and humans both. A rater who sees the machine's verdict first
anchors to it and the kappa is meaningless.

**Arm-level throughput skew.** With six arms, one slow or rate-limited arm can
stall the whole run if concurrency is global. Per-arm concurrency, read from
config.

**Never cut, whatever the schedule:** the truncation guard, the baseline ceiling
check, blinding.

### Reduced-scope fallback

Cut in this order:
1. Arms 6 -> 4 (drop one Claude tier and one open-weight; the design is arm-additive)
2. Iterations 3 -> 1 (single-shot repair)
3. Sites 60 -> 30, stratified 3 per category
4. Human validation 30 -> 20 items
5. Probes down to tab traversal + heading outline only

**Floor version that is still a poster:** 30 sites x 2 pages x 2 conditions
(`wcag`, `both`) x 2 arms, single-shot, axe + accessible-name verbosity, 20
human-rated items. ~240 calls, buildable and runnable in about 5 hours.

## 13. Known limitations (state these on the poster)

- 6 sites per category, so no per-category claims
- 16 of 60 sites are low-form-density; form-related criteria are reported on the
  44-site subgroup
- Repair measured on a 2-page slice of ~11.5-page sites, chosen non-randomly to
  include forms
- axe detects a minority of WCAG criteria; coverage is the enumerated subset in
  section 5, not all of WCAG
- Non-reasoning repair at temperature 0 (deterministic decoding), one run per
  cell with no replicates
- Six repair models spanning **five** families -- Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 share one
- 40 replicas retired before the study (`archive/`); the surviving 60 are
  balanced 2/2/2 by generator group
- Usability judged by an LLM, validated against 3 **novice** raters (the research
  team), not accessibility professionals
- Human validation uses a rotated arm draw: each arm appears in 5 of 30 items, so
  per-arm validity is not estimated
- The 30 validation items are drawn from form-capable sites only, so kappa may
  generalize imperfectly to the low-form-density subgroup
- Baselines built to a WCAG-oriented target -> possible ceiling effect
- Alt-text criterion measures form and contextual appropriateness, not accuracy
- No assistive-technology testing beyond a ~6-page illustrative VoiceOver sample
- `naive` and `nielsen` conditions are retained but the primary contrast is
  `wcag` vs `both`
